How it decides

It tests at least 500,000 designs every run (split over your phone's cores, each core annealing its own share), and keeps the one that best meets “Best means” while passing every design rule. Rockets can grow sideways with up to two rings of boosters, liquid boosters can crossfeed the core (asparagus staging), and landing-and-return trips can use a separate lander that leaves the mothership in orbit. Shuttles put a winged orbiter on an external tank with solid boosters. Rockets are scored stage by stage with the rocket equation: the climb off Kerbin is flown in a 2D simulation with drag (MechJeb's classic ascent profile, drag from frontal area and a Mach curve as in Project-Atmospheric-Drag), and the rest of the trip uses the stock Δv map (then transfer, capture, landing and return), with sea-level Isp low in the atmosphere and minimum thrust-to-weight for each part of the trip (1.25 off the pad, 2.0 for landings). Spaceplanes are flown in a 2D simulation: runway takeoff, jet climb with intake air limits, rocket ascent to 80 km, circularisation. Part figures are from the KSP wiki; a few are estimates and are marked.
